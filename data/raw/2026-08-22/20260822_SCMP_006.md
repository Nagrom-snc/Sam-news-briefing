Alibaba’s AI cloud growth surge drives earnings despite soaring tech spending
Ann Cao and Wency Chen Published: 6:16pm, 20 Aug 2026
 www.scmp.com/tech/big-tech/article/3364705/alibabas-ai-cloud-growth-surge-drives-earnings-despite-soaring-tech-spending

Tech giant posts fastest growth in cloud and AI revenue in 22 quarters, says huge AI computing investments will pay off within three years

Alibaba Group Holding on Thursday reported revenue growth of 45 per cent for its cloud and artificial intelligence division in the June quarter, driving adjusted profit to a better-than-expected 27.3 billion yuan (US$4 billion).

The company assured investors that its soaring spending on AI infrastructure would pay off, with executives noting in an earnings call that its AI computing investments would break even within three years – or possibly two as gross margins rose.

AI Cloud and Compute Services, the Chinese tech giant’s new reporting segment covering its cloud business and T-Head chip arm, posted 48.4 billion yuan in revenue for the three months ended June 30, marking its fastest growth pace in 22 quarters.

The unit’s adjusted Ebita – a measure of profitability, which stands for earnings before interest, taxes and amortisation – rose 133 per cent to 5.6 billion yuan, implying a margin of 11.6 per cent, up from 7.2 per cent a year earlier.

The segment’s AI-related product revenue maintained triple-digit growth for the 12th straight quarter, hitting 12.4 billion yuan, up from 8.97 billion yuan in the previous quarter.

Driven by these AI gains, the Hangzhou-based company’s total revenue increased 9 per cent year on year to 269 billion yuan for the quarter, in line with the Bloomberg consensus estimate of 268.5 billion yuan. That was faster than the 3 per cent growth seen in the previous quarter.

Alibaba’s overall adjusted Ebita dropped by 30 per cent year on year to 27.3 billion yuan, compared with the analyst consensus forecast of 26.6 billion yuan.

Capital expenditure also surged 75 per cent from a year earlier to 67.7 billion yuan, compared with 26.9 billion yuan recorded in the previous quarter.

In an earnings call on Thursday, company executives attributed the rise in spending to procurement timing, the need to add computing capacity to meet expected demand for AI agents, and higher chip prices.

Eddie Wu Yongming, CEO of Alibaba Group, said the company’s AI computing investments typically broke even within three years. As gross margins continued to rise, that time frame could shorten to about two years, he added. Alibaba owns the South China Morning Post.

Free cash outflow in the reported quarter more than doubled to 44.7 billion yuan from 18.8 billion yuan a year earlier, as heavier investment in cloud infrastructure further strained cash generation.

Alibaba’s New York-listed shares slid 2.4 per cent on Thursday following the earnings announcement. Its Hong Kong-listed shares gained 1.6 per cent to HK$126.2 on Thursday, ahead of the release.

The results underscore Alibaba’s strategy of building full-stack AI capabilities – from chips and models to applications – as it seeks to cement its position as a market leader amid intense domestic and international competition.

During the earnings call, Wu highlighted the supernode solutions powered by T-Head’s Zhenwu M890 AI processor, which launched earlier this month at commercial scale.

“We expect supply to continue ramping up in the second half of the year to meet strong customer demand,” Wu said.

The company would “gradually tape out and produce” its second generation of AI chips in the second half of the year, he added, referring to the final stage in chip design. The new chips will be able to perform large-scale AI model training, according to the company.

During the June quarter, the company streamlined its reporting segments in a “strategic alignment” designed to better integrate its commerce and AI businesses. They now report under four main units: Alibaba E-commerce Group, AI Cloud and Compute Services, AI Labs and Applications, and others.

The AI Labs and Applications division – covering its AI model labs, Qwen Consumer Business Group and workplace AI agent platform QwenWork – generated 3.34 billion yuan in revenue, a 16 per cent year-on-year increase.

Annual recurring revenue from model and application services, including models-as-a-service, had exceeded 16 billion yuan, the management said.

“We delivered a strong quarter, driven by the improving commercialisation of our full‑stack AI capabilities,” Wu said in a stock exchange filing.

The company’s full‑stack strategy has put it “in a superior position to capture the substantial growth of demand for artificial intelligence and AI compute”, he said.

Meanwhile, Alibaba E-commerce Group, which combines domestic and international e-commerce operations along with grocery chain Freshippo, recorded a 4 per cent year-on-year revenue increase to 205.9 billion yuan, with an adjusted Ebitda of 39.7 billion yuan.

China Quick Commerce – a category that includes Taobao Instant Commerce, Freshippo and Tmall Supermarket’s on-demand delivery business – saw revenue rise 45 per cent to 53.3 billion yuan.

Jiang Fan, Alibaba’s e-commerce chief, said the business’ user base and order volume continued to grow, while unit economics improved and losses narrowed. The business should turn profitable in the 2029 financial year and become the division’s “second growth engine”, he added.
