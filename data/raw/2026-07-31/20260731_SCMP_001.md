Tim Cook warns of slow Siri AI roll-out in China, worsening memory crisis
Iris Deng Published: 10:31am, 31 Jul 2026
 www.scmp.com/tech/big-tech/article/3362464/tim-cook-warns-slow-siri-ai-roll-out-china-worsening-memory-crisis

In earnings call, Cook says Apple to launch AI software in China, an effort to retain competitive edge in world’s largest smartphone market

Apple is taking its first concrete steps to roll out initial artificial intelligence (AI) features in China, but CEO Tim Cook warned that a full integration of a revamped Siri AI there would take longer.

Mainland Chinese consumers would first get a targeted suite of original Apple Intelligence capabilities, including the Clean Up photo-editing tool, which allows users to erase unwanted objects in photos, according to Cook.

“We are working now through the roll-out of those, and there will be more work required down the road for Siri AI, but we are at the front end of that,” Cook told analysts during an earnings call on Thursday afternoon California time.

The deployment follows Apple Intelligence’s long-awaited regulatory clearance in China earlier this month. The US technology giant is partnering with domestic firms, including Alibaba Group Holding and Baidu, to power the features on iPhones – two years after Apple Intelligence first launched elsewhere. Alibaba owns the South China Morning Post.

The roll-out is critical for Apple, which is leaning on AI features to stave off fierce domestic competition in China – the world’s largest smartphone market. The news of its expansion arrived alongside disappointing regional financial performance.

Apple’s total revenue in mainland China, Hong Kong, Taiwan and Macau – rose 22 per cent to US$18.8 billion for the quarter ended June 27, but still fell short of Wall Street estimates of US$19.6 billion.

Globally, Apple’s revenue rose 16 per cent year on year to US$109.4 billion, driven by a 22 per cent surge in iPhone sales.

However, Apple warned of worsening “supply constraints” for semiconductors ahead in the September quarter, which would affect iPhone, Mac and iPad manufacturing, leading the company to issue weaker year-on-year sales growth guidance of 9 to 11 per cent.

“With less flexibility in the supply chain, we expect the impact from the supply constraints to increase significantly, sequentially,” Cook said, adding that the shortage was driven by stronger-than-expected demand for iPhones and Macs.

Memory costs were expected to be even higher in the September quarter, Cook warned, as the company aimed to offset the pressure with stockpiled inventory amid a global crunch he called a “hundred-year flood”.

“We’ve got a quarter that we’re going to be scrambling on the supply side, essentially,” Cook said in the earnings call, which was his last as CEO before handing over the reins to John Ternus in September.

Last month, Apple increased prices for Macs, iPads, Vision Pro and home devices globally, with some models now costing 20 per cent more amid rising memory and storage costs, while analysts forecasted iPhones to be next.

An ongoing upwards trend in memory prices was expected, creating an even tougher environment for the smartphone industry in the second half, said Tarun Pathak, a research director at Counterpoint Research.

“However, Apple is expected to outperform the industry across its key hardware categories, driven by the strength of its installed base and initial positive reception of Siri AI,” Pathak said. “June quarter results reinforce this view.”
