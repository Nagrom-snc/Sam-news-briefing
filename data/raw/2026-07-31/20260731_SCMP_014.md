Chinese shipbuilders secure new vessel orders as Beijing pushes for high-margin shift
Alice Li Published: 5:13pm, 30 Jul 2026
 www.scmp.com/economy/china-economy/article/3362394/chinese-shipbuilders-secure-new-vessel-orders-beijing-pushes-high-margin-shift

Buyers in both domestic and international markets submit requests for vessels as the industry’s momentum continues

Chinese shipbuilders have secured a string of new orders for large-scale vessels from domestic and international buyers, building on strong order momentum in the first half of the year, amid China’s push to move the industry up the value chain.

Hengli Heavy Industry, a Liaoning-based shipbuilder, has secured construction contracts for six very large ammonia carriers (VLACs) from three international shipowners within one week, its parent company Hengli Group said on Wednesday. Four of the vessels have a capacity of 93,000 cubic metres, while the other two have an 88,000-cubic-metre capacity.

The deals follow Hengli’s successful June launch of the world’s first 93,000-cubic-metre VLAC, built on a slipway rather than in a dry dock, challenging the industry’s long-standing practice of constructing large gas carriers only in dry docks.

Hengli said the breakthrough, combined with its in-house capabilities, had given the company “a differentiated competitive edge ... in areas such as delivery timelines and construction costs”.

Also on Wednesday, Shanghai-based Waigaoqiao Shipbuilding secured a contract from Cosco Shipping Development to build 10 bulk carriers of 210,000 deadweight tonnage (dwt), worth 5.28 billion yuan (US$781.31 million), according to the buyer. The Jiangsu-based Nantong Xiangyu Shipbuilding & Offshore Engineering would build another five vessels for the company under a separate 2.64 billion yuan contract.

The slew of new orders comes amid China’s broader expansion in global shipbuilding, as the country continues to strengthen its position across both traditional and higher-value vessel segments.

In the first half of this year, China’s new shipbuilding orders, a key indicator of industry competitiveness, jumped 173.1 per cent to 121.06 million dwt, surpassing the previous full-year record and accounting for 82.3 per cent of global new orders, according to state-run CCTV on Tuesday.

Some of China’s leading shipyards had order backlogs extending through 2029 and 2030, the CCTV report said.

China maintained its leading position across the three major vessel segments – bulk carriers, container ships and tankers – with new orders in each segment accounting for more than 80 per cent of the global market in the January-June period, the state broadcaster previously reported. The country also expanded its presence in higher-value vessels, with Chinese shipbuilders taking more than 68 per cent of global new orders for new energy vessels.

The momentum comes as Beijing pushed to upgrade the shipbuilding industry towards higher-end manufacturing, identifying the sector as a traditional industry targeted for upgrading over 2026–30 period, according to a document released by the National Development and Reform Commission in late 2025.

As a major shipbuilding hub, Shanghai pledged in a July plan to build a world-class shipbuilding and offshore equipment cluster, focusing on hi-tech vessels, liquefied natural gas carrier capabilities and green and intelligent transformation through low- and zero-carbon technologies.
