Unitree to launch IPO next week as US-China robotics rivalry intensifies
Xiaofei Xu and Wency Chen Published: 12:24am, 31 Jul 2026
 www.scmp.com/tech/tech-trends/article/3362441/unitree-launch-ipo-next-week-us-china-robotics-rivalry-intensifies

The Hangzhou-based firm will launch its listing in Shanghai next week, as a string of Chinese robotics firms pursue plans to go public

Unitree Robotics will launch its initial public offering in Shanghai next week, the firm announced late on Thursday, as China’s robotics start-ups race to go public amid an intense rivalry with their counterparts in the United States.

The Hangzhou-based company plans to sell 40.45 million shares, or 10 per cent of its enlarged share capital.

After the listing, Unitree founder Wang Xingxing and related parties will hold 31.29 per cent of the company’s shares and 65.31 per cent of its voting rights.

Unitree will begin book-building on August 5 and set an offer price the following day, with the final results set to be published on August 14.

One of China’s most high-profile robotics firms, Unitree had its listing application accepted by the Shanghai Stock Exchange in March. China’s securities regulator cleared the registration on July 2.

The company’s accounts show a profitable business in a sector that mostly burns cash. Unitree booked revenue of 1.7 billion yuan (US$251 million) last year and adjusted profits of 591 million yuan.

By contrast, its peer UBTech Robotics – which is listed in Hong Kong – generated revenue of 2 billion yuan over the same period, but made a loss of roughly 700 million yuan.

Unitree’s announcement came days after the United States announced an effective ban on imports of new foreign-made robots, in a move widely seen as a response to China’s rapid advances in the sector.

The ban will apply to new “advanced robotic devices” made in several countries including China, with humanoids, quadrupeds and a broad class of connected mobile machines – including robot vacuum cleaners.

Chinese companies accounted for more than 80 per cent of the humanoid robots installed globally last year, according to a report by Counterpoint Research.

Unitree achieved huge popularity in China earlier this year, when its humanoid robots performed a dance during the country’s Spring Festival Gala – one of the world’s most-watched television shows.

The firm is one of several Chinese robotics firms pursuing plans to go public. AgiBot is preparing for a Hong Kong listing, people familiar with the matter said, and Leju Robotics and Deep Robotics have had listing applications accepted in Shenzhen and Shanghai.
