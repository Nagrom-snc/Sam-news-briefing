Ant Group spin-offs pursue independent growth with strategic capital moves

The Chinese fintech giant’s three spin-off units are accelerating their capital-raising efforts after gaining operational autonomy in 2024

The three independent spin-offs of Chinese fintech giant Ant Group are accelerating their capital-raising efforts, forging distinct financial paths after gaining operational autonomy in 2024.

Ant Digital Technologies is preparing a financing round ahead of its initial public offering, according to Chinese financial news outlet STAR Market Daily, marking the latest strategic move from one of the units that it spun off. Details regarding the round’s valuation, target size, timeline or planned listing venue were not disclosed. The company declined to comment on Thursday.

The pre-IPO push follows a series of capital moves across all three entities.

Ant International, the overseas payments affiliate, recently completed a US$1.2 billion Series A round of financing, although it did not disclose its post-money valuation. Existing shareholders Ant Group and Alibaba Group Holding took part alongside international institutional investors, the company announced last week.

Meanwhile, database developer OceanBase was seeking its first external equity financing, according to multiple Chinese media reports this week. In response, OceanBase said it was focused on data technology and product innovation in the era of artificial intelligence, adding that it would continue to engage openly with capital markets.

These moves show how the three entities are building separate capital structures, allowing them to raise funds independently following Ant Group’s aborted listing in 2020.

In March 2024, Ant said Ant International, OceanBase and Ant Digital Technologies would each establish their own boards and operate independently. The three companies also introduced separate employee share-option programmes designed to better suit their start-up-like status, while Ant Group remained their holding company.

Ant International, which has the largest international footprint of the three units, operates cross-border payment network Alipay+, merchant payment service Antom, digital-account platform WorldFirst and embedded-finance business Bettr. The company said the latest financing round would support its expansion in cross-border payments, digital commerce and AI-powered services.

Ant International also connects with banks, card networks, with mobile-payment providers and technology companies collectively reaching about 150 million merchants and 2 billion consumer accounts, according to the company. It processed more than 2 billion cross-border transactions in its core emerging markets last year.

Ant Digital Technologies, meanwhile, sells enterprise technology based on know-how it developed inside Ant Group, including blockchain, privacy computing, digital identity, risk control, mobile-development tools and cloud-native infrastructure.

The company also recently stepped up its push into enterprise AI. At the World Artificial Intelligence Conference in Shanghai earlier this month, it introduced Agentar 2.0, a platform designed to help companies build and deploy AI agents. The platform included about 200 templates for job-specific “digital experts” and hundreds of ready-to-use tools, according to the company.

OceanBase, which developed the distributed database technology originally created to support Alipay, began establishing a more independent ownership structure in 2024.

Ant transferred its shares in OceanBase to 35 new shareholders through a share-swap arrangement, meaning that the database company was no longer wholly owned by the fintech group. The shareholders included entities linked to Ant employees and investment firms such as HongShan, Yunfeng Capital and China Capital Investment Group.

OceanBase subsequently described itself as an independent software company, while continuing to work with Alibaba Cloud to sell its database products.

Alibaba Group Holding owns the South China Morning Post.
