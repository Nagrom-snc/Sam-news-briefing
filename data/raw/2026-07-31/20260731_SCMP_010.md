John Lee meets business and innovation leaders over Hong Kong’s first 5-year plan
Denise Tsang Published: 8:54pm, 30 Jul 2026
 www.scmp.com/news/hong-kong/hong-kong-economy/article/3362426/john-lee-meets-business-and-innovation-leaders-over-hong-kongs-first-5-year-plan

Chief executive meets members of Council of Advisers, including Sun Piaoyang of Jiangsu Hengrui Pharmaceuticals and StarFive Semiconductor Technology CEO Xu Tao

Hong Kong’s chief executive has met the business and innovation leaders advising him on the city’s strategic direction as part of drafting the first local five-year plan, with one adviser saying he was “inspired” by the high-powered group’s discussions.

Speaking after the string of lunch meetings held over the past three days, Chief Executive John Lee Ka-chiu said on Thursday that the members of his Council of Advisers were confident the city would continue to give “full play” to its roles as a “super value-adder” and a “superconnector”.

He also noted that among those taking part were new members Sun Piaoyang, chairman of China’s largest drug maker, Jiangsu Hengrui Pharmaceuticals, and Xu Tao, CEO of StarFive Semiconductor Technology.

“Faced with a competitive external environment and accelerating technological transformations, Hong Kong must adopt a long-term perspective and formulate more systematic strategies for key development areas,” Lee said.

Lee established the council in 2023 in the wake of the pandemic, seeking to tap business leaders’ insights on the broader goals for Hong Kong’s development.

The latest discussions focused on three major themes: economic advancement and sustainability; innovation and entrepreneurship; and regional and global collaboration.

“By leveraging its status as an international centre, proactively developing into an international innovation and technology [I&T] centre and an international hub for high-calibre talent, members are confident that Hong Kong will continue to give full play to its roles as a ‘super value-adder’ and a ‘superconnector’, thereby better integrating into and serving the overall national development while advancing its own development,” Lee said.

One of the council’s 43 members, Lan Kwai Fong Group chairman Allan Zeman, told the South China Morning Post that Lee was open-minded about the views shared.

“We have a very high-powered group,” he said. “We have good comments from members. I am very happy with the lunch and I am inspired.”

The input of new members Sun and Xu also offered different perspectives especially on technology, which “is key to our future”, he said.

“Technology ... will keep Hong Kong at the forefront,” Zeman said.

According to the government, issues discussed at the sessions included how to further strengthen Hong Kong’s position as an international hub for finance, shipping and trade, while also speeding up the development of the Northern Metropolis and integrating more deeply into the Greater Bay Area.

Another topic was pushing forward with the transformation of traditional industries as Hong Kong evolved into an I&T hub and a magnet for leading global talent, the government said.

Lee is drafting Hong Kong’s first five-year plan, which aims to align the city with the national development blueprint covering 2026 to 2030.

The two-month public consultation ends on August 14 and Lee has pledged to unveil the blueprint later this year.
