Foreign buying of US homes falls 14% as Chinese remain top spenders despite state curbs
Zhu Wenqian Published: 8:00am, 31 Jul 2026
 www.scmp.com/business/article/3362454/foreign-buying-us-homes-falls-14-chinese-remain-top-spenders-despite-state-curbs

The drop in foreign homebuyer activity mirrors the decline in international visitors and tourists to the US, says realty group

Foreign purchases of US homes fell to their second-lowest level on record between April last year and March, although Chinese buyers remained the largest spenders as they bought higher-priced properties primarily in California, a new report found.

Chinese buyers, last year’s top group, fell to third place in terms of home buying volume, behind Canada and Mexico. India and the UK were in fourth and fifth spots, according to a report on international transactions by the National Association of Realtors in the US.

Still, China remained No 1 in dollar volume at US$7.6 billion, indicating an average purchase price of roughly US$1 million, according to the report released on Wednesday.

High prices and tight inventory outweighed a favourable exchange rate. International buyers bought 67,100 properties, down 14 per cent year on year to the second-lowest level since the association started tracking foreign buyer activity in 2009. The median purchase price was US$465,000.

“The decline in foreign homebuyer activity mirrors the decline in international visitors and tourists to the US,” said Lawrence Yun, chief economist at the National Association of Realtors. “Even a slightly weaker US dollar over the past year, which provides more purchasing power for foreigners, did not induce more activity.”

“Foreign buyers from Canada and Mexico – countries that border the US – bought the most housing units. However, Chinese buyers (from mainland China, Hong Kong and Taiwan) spent the most dollars because they bought higher-priced homes, specifically in California,” Yun said.

Florida, with its beaches and favourable winter climate, was the top US destination for foreign homebuyers, with 20 per cent of all international buyers purchasing properties there. The next most popular states were California, Texas, New Jersey and Georgia.

However, Florida has imposed stricter curbs on overseas buyers. Last November, a federal appeal court approved a controversial law that restricted Chinese buyers who were not US citizens or green card holders from buying property in the state, enabling enforcement of the ban.

Separately, a new law in Texas effective in September bans individuals and companies from China, Russia, Iran and North Korea from buying land or property in the state. Authorities said the bill was intended to safeguard national security. US citizens and green card holders are exempt, and holders of valid visas may own one primary residence.

The report also noted that foreign buyers living in the US as recent immigrants or visa holders bought 37,600 homes, representing 56 per cent of all foreign purchases. Foreign buyers who lived abroad acquired 29,500 homes.

Over the 12-month period, foreign buyers bought US$45.3 billion worth of existing US homes, making up 2 per cent of total home sales, down 19.1 per cent year on year.
