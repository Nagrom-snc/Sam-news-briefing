Parts of northern Hong Kong face highest climate disaster risk, study finds
Lo Hoi-ying Published: 9:27pm, 30 Jul 2026
 www.scmp.com/news/hong-kong/health-environment/article/3362429/parts-northern-hong-kong-face-highest-climate-disaster-risk-study-finds

Hong Kong Red Cross urges government to include climate resilience in its long-term development plans

Parts of northern Hong Kong face the highest risk of climate-related disasters, a study by the Hong Kong Red Cross has found, with the charity urging the government to take climate resilience planning into account in developing the area.

A report released by the charity on Thursday also said that more than 50 per cent of the city faced high-risk threats from two or three types of hazards, such as typhoons, extreme heat or fires, simultaneously.

“In planning Hong Kong’s future, this risk map helps all sectors gain earlier insights into regional disaster risks during the initial stages of planning new communities, enabling disaster prevention and preparedness to be integrated into development blueprints,” Hong Kong Red Cross CEO Eleanor Lam Chuen-ping said.

“We hope the government will prioritise supporting facilities in high-risk and rural areas, and include climate resilience in its long-term development plans.”

Using spatial and statistical data from the government, the Hong Kong Observatory and its own public questionnaire survey, the Red Cross ranked different parts of Hong Kong according to 42 factors to assess their vulnerability to extreme weather.

The study split the city into nearly 300 zones based on the government’s Tertiary Planning Units, which are small geographic areas used for town planning and census statistics. The Red Cross said this offered a more refined analysis of the city’s areas compared with the usual 18 districts.

In addition to examining infrastructure, the availability of facilities and geographical features, the ranking also took into account factors such as residents’ income levels and the community’s ability to adapt, withstand and recover from the adverse effects of climate change.

The study found that nearly 15 per cent of Hong Kong faced extremely high disaster risk, with these areas primarily located in parts of Yuen Long and North district.

Other areas at extremely high risk included densely populated urban parts of Kowloon, such as Yau Ma Tei, Sham Shui Po and To Kwa Wan, as well as Tai O on Lantau Island.

The top five highest-risk zones were all in the New Territories – in Lau Fau Shan, San Tin and Pat Heung in Yuen Long, and North district.

Yvonne Yu, head of strategic planning and humanitarian diplomacy at the charity, said that certain areas of the New Territories had experienced a notable number of “very hot days”, which the Observatory defines as days with a maximum temperature of 33 degrees Celsius or above.

Kwu Tung and San Tin in the New Territories experienced the highest number of very hot days last year, at 103 days, the study found.

Yu also noted that places ranked high on the vulnerability list lacked temporary typhoon shelters near residential areas.

“Some temporary shelters are located too far from residential areas, making them difficult to reach quickly during severe weather and preventing them from effectively serving their emergency function,” she said.

Karena Tang, the charity’s manager for planning and service development, said it was hoped the study would serve as a reference point for authorities when planning new developments.

In rural locations, such as Pat Heung, where residential areas are more spread out, authorities could work with community organisations to turn public spaces like churches or schools into temporary shelters to provide better coverage, she said.

“For example, selecting better locations for shelters to be more convenient for residents, and empowering residents with disaster preparedness knowledge,” she said.

“The design of neighbourhoods could ensure more greenery and plants to help mitigate urban heat.”

Some of the areas listed as high risk in the study are set to be developed as part of the government’s multibillion-dollar Northern Metropolis megaproject in the New Territories. These include the San Tin Technopole, an innovation and technology hub, while Kwu Tung North will be developed into a residential area and major transport hub.
