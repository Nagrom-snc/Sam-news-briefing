Page 2: There are a lot more reports on Anutin Charnvirakul’s visit to China on this page.

The editorial choice to do so is really curious. Why would you not carry Li Qiang’s meeting with Anutin and the joint statement on the front page? By any conventional measure of newsworthiness, that’s the bigger story than Zhao meeting Anutin. Instead, the front page carries the report on Zhao’s meeting, while the Li–Anutin meeting is pushed elsewhere. The only logic I can apply is one of editorial hierarchy and political balance. The State Council meeting report clearly mattered more to the editors than the Li–Anutin meeting, so it earns front-page billing. And since Li already has a front-page headline through the State Council report, giving him a second front-page headline would create an imbalance, especially when other PBSC members have done things that also warrant reporting. So the Li–Anutin meeting yields its place, and Zhao’s activity gets covered instead. In effect, what we’re seeing is a managed balance of mentions across the front page, calibrated so that no single PBSC member dominates while others are visible. The telling detail is about Xi Jinping, who gets two headlines despite doing nothing in public.
